# QSME content calendar: 5 Oct – 1 Nov 2026

3 posts a week for each edition. Every post was fact-checked against `research/2026-10-02-qsme-platform-truth.md`.

## QSME NZ (qsme.co.nz)

All times are NZDT (UTC+13; daylight saving began 27 Sep). The weekly rhythm repeats so the audience learns it. A short goes out on Tuesday at 7:30pm, the strongest weeknight for Auckland homeowners, first-home buyers and small developers on IG Reels, TikTok and Facebook (Facebook skews to older renovators, so key shorts also get a 4:5 feed cut). A second short goes out mid-week at a time matched to who it's for: 7:30–8pm for owners, 6:00–6:15am for tradies and site managers scrolling in the ute before the gate opens. The long-form goes out at the weekend, when YouTube and Facebook long-watch and 'how do I…' searches peak: Saturday 9am for section hunters before open homes and owner-builders on Labour Day weekend, Sunday 7pm for people planning their week. The exception is week 4, whose long-form goes out on Labour Day evening (Mon 26 Oct) so its 'your spreadsheet worked Labour Day' cold open lands on the day. Each week follows one build-journey stage: find a site, will it stack up, price, compare and sign, then build and hand over. Every week has one long-form and at least one funny short (Which Dave?, Granny flat for Mum, the thumbs-up contract, the pen on a string). Site research leads week 1 and appears in 5 of the 12 posts (Back corner, Auckland first, Granny flat, Walk-away number, Address to site report). The two week-1 site posts are both filmed on the homepage address search but demo different features: Back corner is the hazard chips; Auckland first is coverage by council, where the chips appear only as a 2-second wide shot pointing back to Back corner. That is a deliberate judgement call. Each long-form is scoped so it doesn't demo its week's short features; it points to them instead. Existing inbox cards are reused, not duplicated: Which Dave? runs as qsme-nz-facebook-group, Which one's cheapest? as qsme-nz-apples-oranges-pineapple, and the CCC paperwork short as qsme-nz-ccc-hunt. Week 1 needs no owner recordings: website captures (homepage address search on 135 Albert St plus Christchurch/Hamilton and Dunedin public buildings), AI b-roll with no people labelled Illustrative, walkthrough recreations labelled 'Recreation · mock data', the 04-project phone still (demo-address header covered) and kinetic type. 'Which Dave?' must be finished by Wed 7 Oct: the reel, static, caption and paid copy are already clear of 'Licence-verified' and 'line by line', but Figma slide 6 still needs fixing, the licence setup must be swapped, and the product beat is rebuilt from recreations. Owner recordings are batched into three demo-account sessions, each with every shot listed: Session A (Site research, 3 Auckland commercial or owner's-own addresses, desktop) by Fri 9 Oct for week 2. Session B (tender, three demo bids, agreements, plan reading with a demo pack, SOQ) by Fri 16 Oct for week 3. Signed-in features are always shown on the web (desktop or phone browser), never implied inside the iPhone app, because phone Site search and native schedule edits aren't confirmed in the store build. Every video ships silent with burned-in captions inside the safe box, plus a timed Kiwi VO script (Mel or the Kiwi bloke, 'Q-S-me dot co dot en zed') for when ElevenLabs is upgraded. Characters are fictional situations, never captioned or voiced as customers or as first-person product testimonials, and no AI-generated person plays a story role (gf-1-idea and tradies-still are out). Nothing shows AI people as customers, fake testimonials, counts, plan prices, time or money saved, competitor names, 'verified tradies', escrow, 'compared line by line', every bid itemised, 'any NZ address', 'no consent needed', 'instant', or AI behaviour beyond the product's own on-screen promises.
