# Procurement routes and who does the QS job (owner notes, 2026-10-14)

Source: pasted by the owner for the G14 explainer.

## 1. Traditional (design-bid-build / consultant-led)
Client → real estate agent (market, feasibility) and the consultant team (architect, PQS, structural/MEP/civil engineers, land surveyors) → tender/award → main contractor → subcontractors.
- PQS: the client's money person: cost plans, Bill of Quantities, tender evaluation, auditing progress payments.
- Main contractor: wins the contract, runs the site, H&S, programme, quality; hires subbies per work package.

## 2. Developer-led / owner-builder (direct subcontracting)
Client/developer acts as the main contractor: consultant team (architect, PQS/cost planner, engineers) + in-house PM/site super + direct trade packages (framing, electrical, plumbing, finishes).
- Why: keep the contractor's margin as profit, speed (early trades start while later packages are priced), direct control. Needs in-house construction and risk expertise.

| | Traditional | Developer-led |
|---|---|---|
| Risk | mostly main contractor | client/developer |
| Cost | higher (margin included) | lower direct cost |
| Client effort | low | high |
| Best for | one-off, complex, hands-off owners | experienced developers, repeat projects |

## Owner's point for QSME
- PQS (client side) generates the tender; the contractor's QS prices it (and procures subbie packages). The PQS is the happiest user.
- Most jobs don't have a QS. Then the architect, the civil engineer, the main contractor or a staff member does the QS job, often not the right way, especially on small projects.
- QSME: a project facilitator, all-in-one, inspired by the QS: run the job as a QS, PM or client, A to Z. The QS is the one who looks after money, value and cost risk.
