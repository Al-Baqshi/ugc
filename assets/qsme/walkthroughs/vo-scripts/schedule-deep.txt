The Schedule deserves a closer look — because this is where the whole build lives. Let's go slowly, from an empty page to a plan you can use again and again.

Start with Import jobs. Bring in the SOQ you priced, use the standard New Zealand build, or one of your own templates.

Pick this project's SOQ. Every job you priced already knows its working days. Tick what you want on site — and leave "link them one after the other" on, so they chain themselves.

Import — and the timeline builds itself. Each job lands after the one before, taking its own days, skipping weekends. The arrows show who waits for whom.

Every job knows three things: when it starts, how long it takes, and when it finishes. You only ever choose the start — on a day, after another job, or alongside one. The finish is worked out for you.

Need something in the middle? Click the day it should start. Scaffolding, two days, alongside the frame — in, without touching anything else.

Open any job to see its order of work. What has to finish first — the one that sets your start date. What waits for it. And the gap to leave — four working days for the slab to cure.

Move a job with nothing after it, and it just moves. Nothing else stirs.

But when rain stops the roof — log a rain day. The roof moves, and everything waiting on it moves too. Anyone booked is asked first. Nothing changes until they say yes.

And the plan can never tie itself in a knot. Try making the frame wait for the plumbing — Q S me stops you. That would make a loop: a job can't wait for something that waits for it.

Happy with how this build runs? Save it as your template — your next project starts ready.

That's the Schedule. Tenders and contracts all land here. One plan. Everyone on the same page. Q S me.
