# «دفتر إمي» (Mum's notebook): every category at once

**Owner brief (2026-11-14):** one longer, more creative script that covers many categories smartly, because Lebalist is "everything here": real estate, food, every category, user-made, "a modernised directory", even government and NGO contacts. No escrow in this one.
**Truth:** every Lebanese home runs on Mum's handwritten phone notebook: the doctor for grandpa's knee, the notary "next to the bakery", the municipality nobody answers at, the charity with the medicines, the restaurant from the engagement, the tow truck that comes at night. Whole lives are in those pages.
**Twist:** the notebook dies (coffee, Thursday morning) and the house stops working. The little sister from LB01 hands Mum her phone: «خدي ماما… دفترك.» Every notebook entry reappears on the phone as a Lebalist category. The categories are never read out as a list; they are the notebook's own entries. «مين كتبلك كل هول؟» «ولا حدا. لبنان كاتبو.» (Who wrote all this for you? Nobody. Lebanon wrote it.) That's the user-made directory, said between the lines.
**Target:** families in Lebanon, and the diaspora who remember the notebook. Reels, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube (16:9 cut). Built to be forwarded to Mum.
**Idea test:** R5 S5 O5 Sh4 = **19/20**. Very ownable: no other app is "the notebook for everything".

**English gloss:** In every house in Lebanon there's the most important book in the world… Mum's phone notebook. It has everything: "the doctor for your grandpa's knee", "the notary… next to the bakery", "the municipality… nobody answers", "the charity with the medicines", "the apartment broker… Mondays only", "the tow truck that comes at night", "the restaurant from Rana's engagement". And "Abu Samir, plumber"… crossed out. Until Thursday morning… a cup of coffee. On the notebook. All of it. And the house stopped. Grandpa without a doctor. My cousin without an apartment. Rana's engagement without a restaurant. And Mum calling the numbers that survived: "Hello? Who's this?" My little sister handed her the phone: "Here, Mum… your notebook." Mum scrolls: doctors, notaries, municipalities, charities, apartments, restaurants, jobs… all near her. Mum: "Who wrote all this for you?" My sister: "Nobody. Lebanon wrote it." Mum: "And Abu Samir?" "He retired, Mum." Download Lebalist.

**Why it's smart:** the categories aren't a list the narrator reads. They're the notebook's entries, and the coffee "deletes" them so the phone can bring them back. It also makes LB01's characters (the little sister, Abu Samir) a recurring family, so the series builds.
