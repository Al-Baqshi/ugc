# Action: chase painter (week 6)

**Situation:** Week 6 progress meeting. For six weeks the minutes have carried the same action: 'Chase painter (1 week late)'. The finish date moved anyway, because of something nobody minuted: a late switchboard holding up the electrical fit-off.

**Truth:** Most programme arguments are about the wrong delay. The loudest late trade usually isn't the one holding up the finish.

**Twist:** The minutes repeat one action six weeks running, and the self-own lands when the programme shows the painter was grey (had room) all along.

## Beats
1. 0–3s HOOK.
2. 3–7s Caption: 'Painter: 1 week late. Finish date: didn't move.' VO: 'Painter's a week late. The finish didn't move.'
3. 7–10s A generic finish date ticks out 9 days. Caption: 'Something else moved it. Nobody minuted that.'
4. 10–15s PRODUCT (real QSME): on the Gantt, tick 'What drives the finish date'. A chain of bars gets purple rings joined by thick arrows. Caption: 'Purple decides your finish date.'
5. 15–19s Drag the painter's grey bar a week right and the last bar doesn't move. Caption: 'Grey has room to move.' VO: 'Shortening a grey task does nothing.'
6. 19–23s Drag the purple electrical fit-off bar one day and the finish moves a day. Caption: 'Purple slips a day, the finish slips a day.' VO: 'Shorten a purple one.'
7. 23–27s The printed A4 'Construction programme' slides across a desk; zoom on 'Any one of them running late moves the whole finish.' Caption: 'QSME calls it "What drives the finish date". You can call it the critical path.'
8. 27–30s End card: Book a 30-minute demo, qsme.build.

## Risks
- Never put 'critical path' on screen as if it were the UI label, and show no float numbers or multi-path analysis.
- The chain is off by default and only works on linked tasks, so set up the demo accordingly.
- Switch off the default NZ Christmas shutdown (23 Dec–11 Jan) on the demo project. Global has no local public holidays.
- A task with someone booked on it is sent for their OK rather than moved. Keep demo tasks unbooked, or show the amber 'waiting' state honestly.
- No percentages, and hide money columns with GST labels.
- Critical-path display is common in scheduling tools, so ownability rests on the plain-words printout.
- The painter is innocent in this story; keep it a self-own by the meeting.
