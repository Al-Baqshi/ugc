# Why six bids never compare (it's the tender, not the subcontractors)

**Situation:** A QS has five bids on a structure package and can't hold any two against each other. One priced drawing revision B and one revision C; one is 'plus tax'; one missed Tuesday's scope change; one arrived as an emailed PDF.

**Truth:** Bids don't fail to compare because subcontractors are sloppy. They fail because the tender let everyone price something slightly different.

**Twist:** Fix the tender, not the bidders: five causes, five mechanisms. The honest end is that QSME lines up the totals and opens each bid's own lines, but a person still reads them and decides. This is the long-form follow-up to 'Six bids. Six formats.'

## Risks
- Never say or show 'compared line by line' or a bidder-by-line matrix. qsme.build's hero and /platform/tendering say it and show a matrix mock, so never capture either.
- Never say 'conforming'. QSME doesn't judge compliance or pick the winner, and 'Lowest' is a tag, not a recommendation.
- Comparison labels still read GST for global orgs, and the 'calc.' note says 15%: crop them and never say GST. The VAT card is our own arithmetic and clearly labelled as an example.
- Drawing references are plain rows; the drawing viewer and mark-up are switched off.
- The subcontractors stay the heroes ('not on them').
