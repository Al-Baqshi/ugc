# One record from estimate to contract

**Situation:** Fourteen months into a build, a payment dispute with the structural steel subcontractor comes down to one question: which number did we agree? The estimate spreadsheet shows one total, the award email another and the signed subcontract a third. Three documents, retyped by three people.

**Truth:** On most jobs the estimate, the tender, the contract and the programme are four files retyped by four people, and the disputes live in the gaps between them.

**Twist:** In QSME the estimate is the document that gets tendered, contracted and built against, so nothing is re-keyed: 'what was priced, what was agreed and what was done are the same thing.' The honest close: QSME keeps the record, while people pick the winner, sign and pay.

## Risks
- Award doesn't sign anything (signing happens in Agreements). Milestones are fixed at award, and an award can't be undone.
- The built-in agreement templates and 'Make the agreement' carry NZ-law and tax wording, so for Global use the org's own PDF via 'Sign it as it is'. Never say 'legally binding everywhere' or claim a qualified e-signature.
- Tax labels on bids and contracts still say GST for global orgs: crop them and never say GST.
- No line-by-line matrix. QSME doesn't pick or recommend the winner.
- Never say 'release the funds'. QSME records payments; it doesn't move or verify them.
- Every signed-in shot needs the owner, and the fictional hook figures need sign-off.
