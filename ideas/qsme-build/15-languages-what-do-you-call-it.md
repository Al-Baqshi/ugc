# One tender document, fifteen languages: what do you call it?

**Situation:** On one international job, in one email thread, a German estimator asks for the Leistungsverzeichnis, a Gulf QS for the جدول الكميات and a Turkish estimator for the metraj cetveli, while a UK QS calls the same thing a bill of quantities.

**Truth:** Construction is the same everywhere but the vocabulary isn't, and software that uses the wrong trade word loses trust before the demo starts.

**Twist:** qsme.build uses each trade's own word in 15 languages and mirrors right-to-left for Arabic and Persian. Then it says plainly that the app itself speaks three languages so far: 'Yours next — tell us.' The Kiwi narrator gives up on pronouncing Leistungsverzeichnis (a self-own).

## Beats
1. 0–8s HOOK: language-switch montage.
2. 8–20s The site's own words, verbatim: 'Construction is the same everywhere… What changes from country to country is the vocabulary — and vocabulary is where trust starts.'
3. 20–38s Montage of the /de, /zh, /ar, /tr and /ja estimating pages, with the trade word highlighted wherever the live page uses it. VO (Kiwi, later): 'Leistungs… I'll let a German say that one.'
4. 38–50s The right-to-left moment: the Arabic and Persian pages mirroring. Caption: 'Arabic and Persian read right to left. So does the site.'
5. 50–60s HONEST CARD: 'The website speaks 15 languages. The app speaks English, Spanish and Arabic, so far.'
6. 60–70s The going-global poster as end card: 'Now speaking 15 languages · Yours next — tell us'.
7. 70–78s CTA: 'Request a language or book a demo →' plus the comment prompt: 'What do you call it where you work?'

## Risks
- Never say or imply the app is in 15 languages. The app is English, Spanish and Arabic.
- Don't claim native or professional review. Have a native speaker sanity-check every word set in type, and only use words captured from the live site.
- Legal pages and the blog are English only, and the Spanish product pages are partly English, so don't show them.
- Expect translation nitpicks in the comments: respond, don't argue.
