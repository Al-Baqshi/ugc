## Earlier pitch (not made): "Who do we even call?"

**Truth (working):** first-timers in New Zealand either can't buy their first home or hesitate to develop, because alone they don't have the capital, the know-how or the right people.
**Twist (working):** you don't have to do it alone. Three or four people who want a home in the same town become the developer together; Baqshi organises and matches them, QSME is the tool, and the right professionals come in before anything starts.
**Product facts (owner, 2026-10-25):** Build Circle, starting in New Zealand. Collect interest, match people with the same interest to build in a specific region or town. Help them find the rules needed on construction sites with the QSME tool, and match them with the right contractor, architect, engineer, project manager and estimator before starting. Baqshi is a consultant: can help first-time developers worldwide; actively engaged in NZ. "If there are 3 or 4 people and we are managing everything, they can build the project."
**Site (baqshi.com/#build-circle, live 2026-10-24):** "Build Circle brings together people who want a home in the same place. As one group you buy the land and build at cost, as your own developer, and the margin a developer would have kept stays with you as equity." Steps: Register → Form a circle → Build at cost → Own the margin. The map and worldwide figures are sample data. "Nothing is bought, built or charged until everyone has seen the full costs."

**Truth:** Every Kiwi friend group has floated 'we should all just buy a section together'. Everyone's keen, and it dies at the same two walls. Nobody knows who to call, and the mates don't all want the same place at the same time (someone always moves to Aussie). Different timelines are the most common cause of co-owner fights. This is the owner's 'hesitating to develop it' moment, and it skips the 2021-style doom.
**Twist:** Build Circle answers both questions the chat never could. First, the people: it matches households on where, what home and when, so one mate leaving isn't the end of it. The three avatars left in the chat dock onto the site's own three-dot ring, so the story becomes the logo. Then, who to call: the contractor, architect, engineer, project manager and estimator join the chat as the literal reply to 'who do we even call?'. The rules and every cost line sit on QSME, and 'your circle makes the calls' pays off the pun. The laugh is the dead chat and the Aussie exit, never the viewer.
