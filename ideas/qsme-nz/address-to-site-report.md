# Three sections, one planner: from an address to a site report

**Situation:** A first-time small developer in Auckland has three sections on a weekend shortlist and one Monday meeting with a planner she pays by the hour. She has to decide which address gets the planner's time, and walk in with the right questions and something on paper.

**Truth:** You pay professionals to answer questions, and most people don't know which questions to ask until after the deposit.

**Twist:** The tool's own last word is 'Confirm with a planner or the council', and the whole film builds to that line on purpose. QSME's job is to get you to the right questions and a one-page report to slide across the planner's desk, not to be the planner. Deadpan narrator, honest limits included.

## Risks
- Overlays, services, hazards, the verdict and the requirements are Auckland only, and the captions say so.
- Not a LIM, title search, valuation or advice. The one-pager isn't official or bank-ready. 'Pro' is a view name, not a paid plan.
- The CV & sales tab is locked, so never mock values. Owner names aren't shown, only the count.
- All three shortlisted addresses must be commercial or the owner's own. Blur anything residential.
- It's rule-based public data, not AI. No competitor names. Say 'in seconds', not 'instant'.
