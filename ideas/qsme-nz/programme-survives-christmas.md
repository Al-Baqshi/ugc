# How a programme survives Christmas

**Situation:** Early October in Auckland: an owner-builder doing four townhouses has a spreadsheet programme that promises the buyer, and the lender, a mid-January handover. The spreadsheet works straight through Christmas Day, Boxing Day, New Year and Labour Day weekend.

**Truth:** Every NZ programme that crosses Christmas is about two weeks optimistic on paper, and everyone knows it except the spreadsheet.

**Twist:** The hero moment is bad news: '12 jobs are planned on days the site is shut', then 'Move them off the days off', and the finish date moves OUT. The film celebrates a later date as the most useful thing your software will do all year. Then it shows what makes that date hold: holidays, booked tradies being asked, the purple chain, and a printed programme you can still say out loud in January.

**Why now:** October is when programmes that cross Christmas get set and buyers get told dates. Labour Day (Mon 26 Oct) is the first long weekend that bites. The shutdown, holidays, purple chain in print and the programme PDF all shipped 22–23 Sep.

## Risks
- Old programmes only move when the user presses 'Move them off the days off'. Never imply they're fixed automatically.
- Regional anniversary days aren't built in. Show adding one, and never claim 'every public holiday'.
- Template durations are estimates. Don't voice the task count or the months as a promise.
- Never say 'bank-approved' or 'bank-ready', or name a bank. Never use 'critical path' as a UI label. No percentages.
- Tradie confirmations are in-app and email only. No SMS.
