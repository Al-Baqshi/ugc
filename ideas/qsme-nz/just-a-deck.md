# Just a deck

**Situation:** An Auckland homeowner in a 1970s cross-lease unit (Flat 2 of 2) wants a 12 m² deck off the lounge before summer, and the builder is coming Saturday to quote. They look up the section first, and the checklist says the other flat owner has to consent: the neighbour they've only ever talked to about the bins.

**Truth:** Cross-lease owners think they own their house. They own a share of the land with next door, and any change to the footprint needs the other owners' consent. Most people only find out when they want a deck.

**Twist:** 'Just a deck. She'll be right.' turns into a chat over the fence with the neighbour you've only discussed bins with. A self-own at the situation, never at the neighbour.

## Risks
- Cross-lease homes are private residences. The card must be cropped so no address or legal description shows.
- The tenure is read from the legal-description wording: a clue, not a title search. Never say QSME 'checked the title'.
- Only Auckland has the full requirements list. Elsewhere it falls back to generic items, so demo on an Auckland address.
- The neighbour is never the butt of the joke. The self-own is on our own awkwardness.
- Recognition is limited to cross-lease owners (a big group in Auckland, but not everyone).
