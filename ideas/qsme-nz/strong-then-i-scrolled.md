# Strong. Then I scrolled.

**Situation:** Thursday night in Auckland: a small developer has a ~209 m² Mixed Use site on a main road on her shortlist and an offer to write on Friday. She runs Site research. The verdict pops up 'Strong development potential', and then, under Constraints, there's a red row: 'Designation · Road Widening (Auckland Transport)'.

**Truth:** The thing that kills a site is never on the flyer. It's one line on a planning map nobody opens until after the deposit.

**Twist:** Status flip: the screen celebrates first ('Strong'), then quietly lists a road-widening designation touching the parcel and a volcanic viewshaft over it. The product doesn't kill the deal. It hands you the first three questions for your planner.

## Risks
- Auckland only. Use only real yield numbers from a real Auckland capture; never mock them.
- Always 'indicatively'. 'Strong' never means consent will be granted; never say 'you can build X'.
- Describe the designation accurately (a road-widening designation touches the parcel). Never claim the council will take the land.
- Use a commercial site, cropped, so the real property's owner isn't identifiable.
- It's rule-based public GIS data, so never call it AI. Say 'in seconds', never 'instant'.
