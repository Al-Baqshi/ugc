# The back corner

**Situation:** Saturday 10am at an Auckland open home: a first-home buyer couple walk a tidy, level section that the listing calls 'sunny and low-maintenance'. Nobody mentions that the back corner sits lower than the rest. Before they go in, one of them types the address into 'Search your address' on QSME, and one hazard chip comes back amber.

**Truth:** At an open home you walk the front lawn and the kitchen. The thing that costs you is in the corner you never walked to, and nobody hands you a flood map.

**Twist:** The hazard chips are checked against the whole parcel, not just the letterbox, so a section that looks bone dry can come back 'Present — check'. The product's honest wording is the punchline: it doesn't say 'this floods', it says check. That's exactly the question to take into the open home.

## Risks
- Film Auckland only and caption 'Auckland first'. Services and hazard chips don't exist anywhere else.
- Never say or imply that 'Clear' means flood-free or that 'Present' means it will flood. The chip says check.
- The story is residential, but the address on screen must be a public or commercial one, labelled 'Demo'. Never a private home.
- We need a public address that genuinely returns an amber chip. Never composite or fake one.
- Not a LIM, PIM or engineering advice. Say 'in seconds', never 'instant'.
- The humour targets the open-home gloss, never the agent or the vendor.
