# Section to CCC: the whole journey

**Situation:** Four townhouses on one Auckland section, run by a first-time developer. In real life that's a sticky note of addresses, a spreadsheet estimate, quotes in three inboxes, a group chat, a paper sign-in book that got rained on, and at the end a shoebox of certificates for the council. We follow one demo project through QSME from the address to the CCC pack.

**Truth:** An NZ build is a relay through a dozen inboxes, and the baton is always a PDF somebody can't find.

**Twist:** The baton never leaves the project. The research sticks to the project, the estimate becomes the tender, the award becomes the contract and the agreement, the contract lands on the programme as the same task, and the shoebox at the start becomes one zip at the end. Callback structure: the film opens with the shoebox tipping out and closes with its lid going back on, empty.

## Risks
- QSME compiles certificates. It doesn't issue them or the CCC, and doesn't lodge with council: the VO says 'you lodge it'.
- The CCC pack and checklist templates are on paid plans, so don't imply they're free.
- Payments are recorded, never moved. No escrow, no 'release the funds', no open-banking shot.
- Gate sign-in is a record, not H&S compliance. Don't use the walkthrough audio or show any removed feature (Book a tradie, Quick Jobs).
- No shots of the public tender board or directory, which are nearly empty. Bidders are invited demo contacts.
- Demo project and fictional people only. Never imply that QSME manages the build.
