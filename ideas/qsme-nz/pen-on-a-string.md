# The pen on a string

**Situation:** A builder on a two-townhouse job in Christchurch. The site sign-in book lives in a plastic sleeve on the gate, it rained Tuesday, the pen on the string has gone missing again, and Thursday's page is one smudge. Then someone asks who was on site Thursday.

**Truth:** The paper sign-in book is wet, missing its pen, and useless the one time anyone actually needs it.

**Twist:** An object documentary: a lost-cat-style 'MISSING: site pen' poster on the fence gets cut down, and a laminated QR poster is cable-tied in the same spot, following the product's own instruction: 'Print it, laminate it, cable-tie it to the fence.' The callback: the pen is still missing, mind.

## Risks
- No WorkSafe or Health and Safety at Work Act compliance claims. It's a record, not a lock.
- No GPS, sign-out times or identity verification claims.
- The QR code opens a web page, not the iOS app. Don't show the app.
- Only invented names on any register. QR sign-in apps are common, so 'ownable' depends on the gag, not the feature.
- The owner must film it (week 2+).
