# Granny flat for Mum (Flat 2 gets a vote)

**Situation:** A Kiwi family in Auckland want a 60 m² granny flat in the back yard so Mum can move closer. They've heard the new granny-flat rules mean 'no consent'. Their place is Flat 1 on a cross-lease. One of them opens Site research on the section the night before ringing a builder.

**Truth:** Everybody's heard granny flats got easier. Nobody's mentioned that on a cross-lease, the people next door still get a say.

**Twist:** The good news and the catch arrive in the same checklist. One row says 'Granny flat without consent… can often be added… where the site meets the conditions'. Then, in red, 'Other cross-lease owners' consent', because: Cross-lease title. The deciding vote belongs to Flat 2. Better bake something.

## Risks
- Keep the conditional wording on screen ('can often… where the site meets the conditions'). Never say 'build a granny flat with no consent'.
- Both rows must genuinely appear for a real Auckland site. If no single site shows both, cut them as two sites and don't imply they're one.
- Tenure is read from the legal-description wording, which is a clue, not a title search. Never say QSME checked the title.
- Residential privacy: cards only. No address, map or legal description on screen.
- Don't use the homepage scroll-story line 'Up to 70 m² with no building consent'.
- The joke is the cross-lease situation, never the neighbours.
