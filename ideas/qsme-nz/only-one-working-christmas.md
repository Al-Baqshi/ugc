# The only one on your job working Christmas

**Situation:** In October, a small developer plans a two-townhouse job in a spreadsheet and promises the buyers a 14 January handover. The spreadsheet counts Labour Day, Christmas Day, Boxing Day and the whole 23 Dec–11 Jan shutdown as ordinary working days.

**Truth:** Every NZ builder knows nobody is on site between Christmas and mid-January. The programme is the exception: it works straight through and finishes two weeks early on paper.

**Twist:** The spreadsheet is personified as the keenest worker on the job: 'Worked Labour Day. Worked Boxing Day. Didn't even go to the bach. She'll be right.' QSME then does what everyone actually does and shuts the site 23 Dec to 11 Jan.

## Beats
1. 0-3s HOOK: a generic spreadsheet grid (fictional tasks). The '25 Dec · Framing · day 14' cell glows and gets a marker Santa hat. VO (Mel, later): 'Only one on your job working Christmas Day? Your spreadsheet.'
2. 3-8s: escalation as cells tick 'worked' one by one: Labour Day, Boxing Day, 1 Jan, 2 Jan, 6 Jan… Caption: 'Didn't even go to the bach.' VO: 'Worked Labour Day. Worked Boxing Day. Didn't even go to the bach.'
3. 8-11s: its promise in bold, 'HANDOVER: 14 JAN. She'll be right.', cuts to a quiet site still with a handwritten gate sign 'Back on the 12th. Merry Xmas.'
4. 11-18s: owner recording, QSME schedule, then Days off: 'Christmas shutdown — Shut 23 December to 11 January, every year.', then the amber '12 jobs are planned on days the site is shut', then 'Move them off the days off'. The bars jump past 11 January and the finish date moves out. VO: 'Q-S-me shuts the site twenty-third to the eleventh. Like everyone actually does.'
5. 18-23s: an amber band over Labour Day, and a five-day task stretches past it. The concrete pour gets 'Works public holidays' switched on and only that bar sits on the holiday. Caption: 'National holidays in. The pour's your call.'
6. 23-27s: end card: 'Give the buyers a date that's real.' Sign up at qsme.co.nz. VO: 'Give them a date that's real.'

## Risks
- Old programmes only move when the user presses the button. Never imply an automatic fix.
- Regional anniversary days are not included, so say 'national public holidays', never 'every public holiday'.
- The Schedule may sit in a paid module, so no pricing or plan lines about it.
- No bank names and no 'bank-ready'.
- The spreadsheet must be generic: no competitor or brand UI.
