## One-liner
Global property consultants based in New Zealand, working in 90+ countries. We look after people who want to expand, by buying, building or investing in property in another country, from the first question to the registered title or the keys. https://www.baqshi.com · Tagline: "Own property anywhere on Earth."

## Audience
- Property investors anywhere who want to own in another country ("from X, want to own in Y"). The owner calls them "people who want to expand" (2026-10-20). ❓ Does "expand" also cover businesses opening in a new country, or developers?
- Secondary: sellers who want a transfer that doesn't depend on trust, and developers who want an open-book, quantity-surveyed build.
- Global and English-reading, often as a second language. Mostly on phones, across many time zones. Where they scroll: ❓
- What they already believe: buying abroad means trusting strangers and paying first.

## Pains (in their words)
- "A cross-border property move usually means five strangers in two countries." (site)
- "Can someone from my country even own there? In my name, a company or a trust?"
- "What will it cost, how long will it take, and what do I end up with?"
- "Is it worth building there, or should I just buy?"
- "Who do I pay first, and how do I know they're real?"

## The payoff
One consultant, every step of the route. The plan is in writing before any money moves, and nobody pays first.

## Value props (site wording)
- Find: a shortlist that fits the brief and budget, with a view on yield and exit.
- Qualify: what someone from your country needs to own there, which structure, the costs and the timeline, in a written route plan before any money moves.
- Connections on the ground: a licensed local lawyer or notary for every deal, and the government channels each country needs. Real estate developers ❓: the owner's word (2026-10-20), not on the site. Keep them generic: never named, never "partners".
- Buy or build: land and build cost against what it's worth there, "so the margin is a number you have seen, not a hope". Always marked illustrative, not a forecast.
- Build: the budget is priced line by line on QSME and released straight to whoever did the work.
- Transfer model: the buyer's funds and the seller's title go into one escrow held in New Zealand and are released together. Nobody pays first, nobody signs first. Each deal's terms are set in its escrow agreement.
- Payment layer: in development with QSME on BitRegalo. It is on test networks only and not independently audited.

## Owner rule: escrow, said generally (2026-10-20)
Baqshi content does **not** name BitRegalo and never mentions test networks, audits or any protocol status. Describe the payment side generally: money is held in escrow; a held payment can only go to the person it's for, or back to whoever paid; each deal's escrow agreement sets out exactly how. (Owner: "Don't mention BitRegalo… just mention it generally. We could use BitRegalo or escrow… I don't accept [the test-network line].") Still never claim the escrow is decentralised, on-chain, audited, guaranteed or risk-free, and keep the buy scene's "How the model works… Not legal, tax or financial advice" footnote.

## Do not claim
- That the escrow or BitRegalo is live, launched, on a main network, audited, secure, guaranteed or risk-free. Always say: in development, test networks only, not independently audited.
- Any chain name, token, coin, crypto payment or stablecoin. Funds arrive by ordinary bank transfer, and BitRegalo has no token.
- That BitRegalo holds title, swaps funds for title, uses more than one key, or returns money automatically.
- That QSME holds money, runs escrow or does smart contracts.
- That Baqshi holds or controls client funds. Keep "no single party can move the funds" out of short videos: it sits badly next to BitRegalo's buyer-only release.
- "Nobody carries money across a border": funds still travel by international bank transfer.
- Named lawyers, notaries, banks, registries, agencies or developers, or anyone as an "official / certified / accredited partner".
- Legal, tax, financial or investment advice, or licences. No guaranteed eligibility, approvals, timelines, residency, visas, returns or margins.
- New Zealand as objectively "the safest place". Say where the escrow is held, as Baqshi's choice.
- Any number except 90+. No testimonials, case studies or client stories, and no AI people presented as clients.
- Illustrative map or globe activity presented as real deals.
- The word "Petrigallo".
