# Humor playbook

Funny UGC comes from **truth + specificity + timing**, not from jokes.

## Where the laugh comes from
- **Painful recognition** — show the exact behavior people do but never say out loud.
- **Escalation** — take a small real thing and push it one step too far (then one more).
- **Contrast** — expectation vs reality, what I say vs what I mean, me at 9am vs me at 3pm.
- **Personification** — the object/app/problem as a character with an attitude.
- **Deadpan** — treating an absurd situation completely seriously.
- **Status flip** — the confident one loses, the underdog wins, the product "saves" someone who didn't ask.
- **Callback** — the payoff echoes the hook.

## Rules
- Punch at the situation, the problem, or ourselves — never at the customer.
- The joke must work with sound **off** (captions/visuals) and **on**.
- One joke per video done well beats three half-jokes.
- The product should be *part of* the joke or its resolution, not interrupt it.
- Cut the setup. Start as late as possible.
- End on the laugh or the product payoff, not a long CTA. Organic: 1-line CTA max. Paid: the CTA is required and explicit, but still one sentence in the character's voice.
- In paid videos, the humor hooks and holds attention. It must hand off cleanly to problem → solution, never bury it.

## Checks before we ship
1. Say the joke in one sentence. Can't? It's not a joke yet.
2. Is there a specific detail a stranger wouldn't think of? (That's what makes it real.)
3. Would it still be funny to someone who doesn't know the brand?
4. Could it offend the target audience? Check the venture's humor limits.
5. Cultural fit: does it land in the venture's market/language? Wordplay rarely translates.

## Voice notes
Record the humor that the user actually laughs at in each venture file under "Humor that works". Taste beats theory — update these files over time.
