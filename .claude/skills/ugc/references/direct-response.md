# Direct response: paid UGC that converts

Every video is either **organic** or **paid**. Decide which before writing, because it changes the structure.

| | Organic | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Awareness, community, filling the feed | Sales, tracked per view with ad spend behind it |
| Measured by | Views, comments, saves, shares | Clicks, conversions, CPA/ROAS |
| Pays back | Slowly, over 3–6+ months | Immediately, or it gets switched off |
| Shape | Loose: trend, skit, day-in-life, ASMR | Fast pace, problem → solution, clear CTA |
| Humor role | Can be the whole point | Serves the sale: it hooks and holds, then hands off to the product |
| Length | 15–30s | 30–40s |

## Ad anatomy (paid)
Mix the order freely. **Bold** = required in every paid video.

1. **Hook** (0–3s): stops the scroll. See Hook layering below.
2. **Product intro**: how it improves their life, in as few words as possible.
3. Education (optional): teach the problem so the pain feels bigger before the fix.
4. **Problem → solution**: name the pain in the audience's words, then show the product fixing it.
5. **Value props**: features turned into benefits, and why this beats the alternatives.
6. Social proof: reviews, before/after, numbers, press.
7. **CTA**: push the sign-up (QSME: never "free", "no sign-up" or pricing generosity — see the venture's CTA rule). Say exactly what to do next ("tap the link, the first box is 40% off").

## Paid video types
- **Testimonial**: my problem → found this → what changed → results → CTA.
- **Problem → solution**: your problem → here's the fix → why it's better → CTA.
- **Demo**: show it working → features as benefits → CTA.

Humor goes on top of these. A POV skit can be the hook and the problem, then drop into a demo for the value props and CTA.

## Hook layering
Strong paid hooks stack **3+ layers at once** in the first 3 seconds:
- **Text**: on-screen copy that teases the payoff.
- **Verbal**: the spoken first line (call-out, pain, FOMO, curiosity).
- **Visual**: exaggerated expression, split screen, odd object, handwriting, slow-mo, transition.
- **Audio**: a satisfying or unexpected sound (ding, crunch, ASMR, silence).
- **Seemingly unrelated**: the voiceover and the footage don't match, and curiosity fills the gap.
- **Organic**: a natural in-progress action (making coffee, doing makeup) so it doesn't read as an ad.

Every hook must be: **emotional** (humor, curiosity, pain, excitement), **specific** to the target, **urgent or curious**, and **short**.
- Weak: "My night routine got so much better."
- Strong: "This is the night routine that gave me Rapunzel hair."

## Research before scripting
- **Meta Ad Library** and **TikTok Creative Center**: what competitors run, and which ads have been live longest (long-running usually means it converts).
- Search the niche on TikTok: note recurring hooks, complaints in the comments, and gaps nobody covers.
- Mine real reviews (ours and competitors', especially 2–3 star ones) for pain in the customer's own words.
Save findings in the venture file under "Research".

## Integrity rules
- Social proof must be **real**: reviews, numbers or results the user provides. Never invent testimonials, star ratings or customer counts.
- An AI-generated person presenting a "my experience" testimonial in a paid ad may need disclosure depending on platform and market rules. Flag it to the user when a concept relies on one; don't decide silently.
- Claims (health, money, results) must be ones the venture can back up.

## Script tone
Talk like you're telling your best friend about it. No jargon, no announcer voice. On-screen text shows the key points as short bullets. Plan every shot, then generate extra takes and B-roll so the edit has options.
