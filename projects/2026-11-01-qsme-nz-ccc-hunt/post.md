# Everything's finished. Except one bit of paper.

**The joke / insight in one sentence:** The house is painted, the carpets are down and the family wants to be in by Christmas, but the Code Compliance Certificate is stuck on one missing Record of Work, and the roofer who has it is flat out on a job in Queenstown.

**The joke:** an escalating hunt for one piece of paper, read like a flat incident report: "SEARCH 1 · Inbox." (No results), "SEARCH 2 · Garage." (BOX 3 OF 7: an oven warranty, tile samples, kitchen quote v3, paint colours), "SEARCH 14 · The ute." (a mince-and-cheese pie receipt, "Not it.", "(good pie though)"). Then three polite texts, Monday, Wednesday, Friday (the last one "Any chance? (sorry, me again)", so the owner is the slightly sheepish one), to a contact saved as "Roofer · usually replies Sundays", and the caption "He's on a job in Queenstown. Fair enough." The paperwork is the joke, never the roofer: he's busy, and "fair enough" is meant. The turn is the lesson for the next build: the paper goes in while they're still on site. The roofer records the certificate on the project, you check it and tick the step, and the CCC Pack is one zip with every certificate plus the council index. The honest close: "You still lodge it with council." / "QSME just ends the hunt." (the end-card small print keeps "QSME doesn't issue certificates or the CCC"). The end card calls back to the hook under a "NEXT BUILD" kicker: "Everything's finished. Even the paper.", with the red ✗ on "Record of Work — Roofing" flipping to a green ✓.

**Job:** organic, week 4 of the calendar, the handover chapter. It goes out Sunday 1 Nov at 7:30 pm, which is owner-builders' planning time (and pays off "usually replies Sundays"), right as the pre-Christmas move-in rush starts and everyone wants their CCC before the shutdown. The CTA pushes sign-ups (owner rule 2026-10-03): **Sign up at qsme.co.nz**. It reuses the inbox card `qsme-nz-ccc-hunt` (the same escalating hunt and glovebox gag), moved from "three years later, the lawyer asks" to the CCC application now, so the product fixes the next build rather than promising anything about old paperwork.

## Music and sound direction

- **Shape:** the hook has no music. It's just four rising ticks, then a dull thunk and a marker squeak on the red line. Under the hunt, a dry "searching" bed: muted pizzicato strings and a soft woodblock at about 100 BPM, building one layer per search (inbox, garage, ute). Each "Not it." is a felt-mallet thud and the bed stops dead for a beat. Under the thread, near silence: three soft send whooshes and room tone, so "Fair enough." lands quietly. On "NEXT BUILD" the bed comes back warmer (Rhodes plus light brushed kit, same tempo) and carries the product beats. It drops to a low pad under "You still lodge it with council.", hits once on "QSME just ends the hunt.", and resolves on a held chord on the end card. No logo sting.
- **SFX list:** four rising ticks, a thunk, a marker squeak, five whips, keyboard typing (mail search, dialog fields), a soft error bonk, a box thump and cardboard flaps, four paper flicks, two stamp thuds, a glovebox latch and drop, a paper rustle, three send whooshes, mouse clicks, a soft success chime (toast), a checkbox click, a zip "pop", a paper slide, a low hit, a final tick. All generic: never an iOS keyboard click, iMessage "sent" sound or any platform's notification sound.
- **Source:** one commercially licensed bed for every version, from ElevenLabs Music on a plan that permits commercial use (prompt: *"dry deadpan comedy, muted pizzicato strings and soft woodblock, 100 BPM, no vocals, builds one layer at a time, stops dead for one beat, then turns warm with Rhodes and brushed kit, resolves on a soft held chord, 32 seconds"*), or a business-tier Epidemic Sound / Artlist licence. SFX from ElevenLabs SFX. No in-app music. Keep prompts and licence receipts in `outputs/licences/`.
- **Mix:** VO about 30 LU above room tone; the bed 12–15 dB under the VO.
