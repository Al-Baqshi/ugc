# The Transposition (a trailer)

**The joke in one sentence:** Bid F (the whiteboard from "Six bids") said 71,300, the subcontract says 73,100, and it took seven months of "RE: RE: RE: FW: Contract sum query" to find out why: the figure was typed from an email, which was typed from a spreadsheet, which was typed from a photo of a whiteboard. It's all played as a deadly serious blockbuster trailer.

**The insight:** Every retype is a fresh chance to swap two digits. In QSME the award creates the payment contract from the bid itself, and the same task on the programme carries that figure, so nobody types it again between "we're going with Bidder F" and the contract.

**Job:** Organic, week 2 opener (award week), in the Tuesday AU/NZ seeding slot. It picks up exactly where "Six bids. Six formats." ended (Bid F won), and no other post this week uses its feature (award → contract → the same programme task). It goes out from the founder's LinkedIn profile, the QSME page reposts it at 07:30 London, and a 9:16 re-layout of the same cut goes to YouTube Shorts. The trailer format is the most shareable thing we've made for this audience: every QS and contracts manager has a transposition story.

## Hook layers (chosen: pattern break + mid-action, in trailer grammar)

- **Text (frame 1):** "IN A WORLD…" over a cold, graded dawn shot of a timber frame inside letterbox bars. "WHERE THE BID SAID" lands at 0.32 and **71,300** slams in at 0.74 (240px mono), with the chip "BID F · THE WHITEBOARD" (the Six bids callback) at 1.25.
- **Visual:** cinema letterbox bars inside a 4:5 feed post, silver trailer type, film grain, a white flash frame and a lens-flare streak on the slam. It doesn't look like a LinkedIn post, which is the point.
- **Verbal (VO later):** "In a world… seventy-one three." (the card carries "where the bid said"; at a real trailer pace the full sentence doesn't fit the 3.2s hook)
- **Audio:** a trailer braam on the slam (0.74), then a low drone.
- **Why it works:** "In a world…" is the most recognisable opener in film. Paired with a contract figure, it tells a QS in under a second that this is about them and that it's a joke. The payoff, the 1 and the 3 physically swapping places, comes 3.7s later, so it holds them through the first scroll decision.
