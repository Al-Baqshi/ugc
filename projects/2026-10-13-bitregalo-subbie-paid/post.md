# B02 · The main contractor went bust

Spoken: outputs/vo-take/take-text.txt (40.9 s). Claims fact-checked 2026-10-13 against bitregalo-contracts (EscrowVault.sol release / releaseWithCode / cancel / expire) and bitregalo.com by a 4-agent workflow:
- Escrow alone does NOT pay the sub if the main contractor goes silent: only the buyer can release, and after the deadline the payment can only go back to the buyer.
- The release code (v3, built) set at award and held by the client lets anyone holding it pay the sub before the deadline, without the contractor. That is what the video shows.
- No legal claim about insolvency; on-screen: not legal advice, insolvency law varies by country; test network; not audited; illustrations, made-up names (Harbour Build / Kauri Framing, the site's own example pair).

Description:
Stage two's done. The framing's up. And the main contractor just went bust.

Same job on BitRegalo: before work starts, the contractor locks the stage money into a public contract. The subcontractor can check it's there, and nobody can spend it on anything else. The release code goes to the client: when the stage is signed off before the deadline, the subcontractor scans it and is paid, with no contractor needed. (Not released by the deadline? It can only go back to the contractor.)

And everything is filed under the project: the request for proposal, every bid, who won, every payment. Written once. Nobody can edit it.

One use case of many: marketplace orders, tenders, aid paid on delivery, gifts abroad, memberships and credits.

Try it on the practice network: https://www.bitregalo.com

BitRegalo runs on BNB Smart Chain Testnet, a public test network where tokens have no value, and has not been independently audited. Names and amounts are made-up examples. Not legal or financial advice: how insolvency law treats held payments depends on your country.
